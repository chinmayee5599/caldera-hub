How it works

Two doors, one network.

Come in with GitHub

  • Your repos become a city. Districts are projects, buildings are repos, and lit windows are this week's commits.
  • Watch your sites live. Add one line to a site and its visitors parachute into your city as they arrive.
  • See your AI agents work. Coding agents show up as drones over the repo they are working in.
  • Meet people. Add friends, join a group, and get a daily summary by email.

No install. Your page rebuilds from GitHub every 6 hours.

Come in with Obsidian

  • A living profile. Built on your device from your vault and refreshed every day: a journal, an activity score, streaks and topics.
  • Your story. A timeline, projects with live links and your AI usage, written by your AI from your vault.
  • Private sharing. Send any note to a friend, end-to-end encrypted. It lands in their vault as a normal note.
  • Help each other. Post asks and offers, and give gravity to the people who show up for you.

Free on Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone and Android.

Private by default

  • Public repos only. A GitHub sign-up shows your public repos. Private repos appear only if you install the Caldera GitHub app on the ones you pick, and you choose how they show: padlocked buildings with no names, one anonymous padlocked district, or not at all.
  • We never read your code. Caldera reads repo names, languages and commit counts, never the files inside.
  • Leave any time. Delete your page from your account and Caldera's access to your GitHub is revoked.
  • Your vault never uploads. With Obsidian, Caldera builds your profile on your device and sends only the finished page.
  • You choose every section. Each part of the profile has its own switch. A note is public only if you add caldera: public to it.
  • Your journal is filtered. Every journal line passes a privacy filter before it is published: names you have not made public, meetings, places and anything that looks private are taken out.
  • Shared notes are end-to-end encrypted. The hub only stores ciphertext it cannot read.
  • No telemetry. The plugin talks only to the hub you choose, only when you act.

## 6. Privacy rules (these override everything above)
Everything public is on the open internet, can be copied forever, and can affect other people. Be very cautious. Never write any of these into anything public:
- Other people: no names, initials, handles, photos or descriptions that identify anyone I meet, work with, hire, date, advise or invest with. Say "a founder", "the team", "a friend" only when it adds nothing identifying - usually leave it out.
- Meetings: no meetings, calls, lunches, dinners, interviews or events - not their titles, times, dates, attendees, or that they happened with someone specific.
- Times and places: no times of day, schedules, travel, cities, venues, addresses, offices, home or "where I am".
- Secret or sensitive work: no unannounced, stealth, confidential or NDA projects, codenames, deals, fundraising, investors, acquisitions, valuations, hiring, firing or anything said in confidence.
- Things that can get someone in trouble: legal, medical, health, HR, money, salaries, debts, relationships, family, children, politics, religion.
- Proprietary methods: no trading strategies, signals, indicators, thresholds, regime or risk rules, entry/exit logic, win rates, model weights, prompts, data sources or other trade secrets - in the journal, my notes or anywhere public. Name the theme only ("worked on the trading system").
- Contact details and links: no emails, phone numbers or links to private documents. No money amounts at all.
Describe work only by its public theme ("shipped a release", "wrote about design") and only name things I have already made public on my page (my own public projects, topics and links). If you are unsure, leave it out - an empty field is fine. Caldera also drops any journal line that looks sensitive before publishing.
Themes and topics: only use topics among the tags I allow-listed in Settings -> Caldera -> Public topics.
Also: never write to any other part of the vault, never change notes' privacy markings (caldera: public / friends / private) without asking, never touch .obsidian/plugins/caldera/data.json - it holds my private key.

Prefer to install by hand?

1 · Get the plugin

  1. No Obsidian yet? Download it free from obsidian.md, open it, and choose Create new vault.
  2. Or download caldera-latest.zip.
  3. In your vault, open the hidden folder .obsidian/plugins/ and create a folder named caldera. On a Mac, Cmd+Shift+. shows hidden folders in Finder.
  4. Unzip into it: you should see main.js, manifest.json and styles.css.
  5. Restart Obsidian, then Settings → Community plugins → turn on Caldera.

Easier: Settings → Community plugins → Browse → search Caldera, or open it in Obsidian.

2 · Set it up

  1. Restart Obsidian. The Welcome to Caldera window asks for your name and a handle, publishes your page and opens it in your browser.
  2. Save your recovery key when it offers (Settings → Caldera → Export recovery key any time).
  3. Change anything later in the note Caldera/profile.md; see the Guide.
  4. Open the flame icon panel to add friends and give gravity.

Questions

Do I need Obsidian?

No. Sign up with GitHub and you get a page and a city with nothing to install. You can move into Obsidian later and keep your page.

What does Caldera read from my GitHub?

Your profile (name, bio, avatar, links), your public repos, their languages and weekly commit counts, and your public activity. Private repos only if you install the Caldera GitHub app on the repos you choose. It has read-only access to repository metadata, so it can count activity but cannot open your files. Visitors see private repos only as padlocked buildings, with no names.

I use Obsidian. Do I need it already?

No. Obsidian is a free notes app (obsidian.md) for Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone and Android. The install prompt checks for it, installs it if you say yes, and creates your first vault (a folder of notes). Your page starts simple and fills in as you write.

Is it free?

Yes. Your page, your city, the plugin and friends are free and open source. Optional paid features (like a hosted AI journal and custom domains) may come later.

Who can see my notes?

Only what you publish. Your vault is read on your device; Caldera publishes the sections you switch on and notes you mark caldera: public. Notes you share with a friend are encrypted so only that friend can read them.

What are help credits?

Points you earn by helping people with their asks. Everyone starts with 100. They have no cash value and can't be bought or sold.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes, the plugin runs on Obsidian desktop and mobile.

Can I host it myself?

Yes. Use Export profile to a vault folder to get a static site for any host, or run your own hub. The protocol is open.

How do I delete my profile?

GitHub sign-up: on your account page, choose Delete my Caldera profile; your page is erased and Caldera's access to your GitHub is revoked. Obsidian: run Caldera: Delete my profile from the hub. Your page, friendships, asks, offers, gravity and credits on the hub are erased. Your vault is never touched.

What if I lose my device?

Your identity is a key in your vault's plugin settings. Export the recovery key when you set up, and import it on a new device.
