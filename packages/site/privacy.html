Privacy Policy

Effective October 18, 2026. Caldera (caldera.social, the Caldera hub and the Caldera plugin) is provided by mjonesone, LLC ("we", "us", "our").

The short version
  • Your Obsidian vault stays on your device. The plugin reads it locally and sends only what you choose to publish.
  • If you sign up with GitHub, we read your public GitHub profile and repositories, and only the metadata of private repositories you choose to connect. We never read the code in private repositories.
  • Your email is used only for sign-in links, the daily summary you opt in to, and a short email when someone messages you (you can turn that off). It is stored encrypted.
  • No ads, no tracking cookies, no data sales. Visitor counts use hashes that change every day.
  • You can delete your account and the data tied to it at any time from Settings (or from Obsidian).

What changed on October 18, 2026

We updated this policy to cover sign-in with email links, the daily summary email, member email addresses from GitHub (with your permission), topic tags we derive from your public repositories, friend suggestions and introductions, kudos and view counts, the Feed, direct messages and new-message emails, invitations you send, weather on the city view, nightly backups kept 30 days, search engines and AI assistants indexing listed members' public pages (you can turn it off in Settings; members who joined before October 18, 2026 were given 7 days' notice), public previews of GitHub accounts as cities (caldera.social/gh), and the service providers we use for these.

1. What the plugin does on your device

The Caldera plugin for Obsidian reads your vault on your own device to build your profile: counts (notes, words, links, active days), the tags you allow-list, the fields in Caldera/profile.md, journal lines and counts in Caldera/activity.json, and local AI-tool usage logs you have on that computer (token counts by model only). This happens locally and the plugin has no telemetry. It contacts a hub only when you publish, sync, or take an action (for example a friend request, a shared note, kudos, an introduction or a post).

Your identity key is created on your device and kept in the plugin's settings in your vault. We never receive it. If you move a GitHub sign-up into Obsidian, the hub hands its key to your vault once and deletes its copy.

We do not collect phone numbers, contacts or payment information, and we do not use advertising or cross-site tracking.

Messages

When you message another member, we store the conversation (who wrote to whom, the text and when) so both of you can read it. Messages are text only. They are encrypted on our servers with a key that stays on those servers; they are not end-to-end encrypted, and Caldera staff can access a conversation only to investigate an abuse report or to meet a legal obligation. When you report a conversation, we keep an excerpt of its recent messages with the report so it can be reviewed. We never show your email address to other members, we never sell or share messages, and we never use them for advertising or to train AI models. When someone messages you and you haven't read it within a minute, we email you a short preview (up to 200 characters) with a link back to Caldera, at most once every 30 minutes per person; unread messages can also appear in your daily summary. You can turn message emails off on the Messages page or with one click in any of them.

Messages are kept until either person deletes the conversation (which removes it for both of you), or until an account is deleted: deleting your account erases the messages you sent, and the other person sees "Deleted user". Blocking someone stops their messages from reaching you; they aren't told.

3. Emails we send

  • Sign-in links and confirmations, when you ask for them.
  • New-message emails, when another member messages you: sent to your summary address, or to your verified GitHub email if you granted that permission. One click in the email, or the checkbox on the Messages page, turns them off.
  • The daily summary, only if you opt in. It goes out only on days something happened, and every email has a one-click unsubscribe.
  • Invitations, only when a member presses "Email invite" for someone in their GitHub circle, and only to an address that person shows on their public GitHub profile. Limits apply, every invitation has a one-click unsubscribe, and unsubscribed addresses are never emailed again (we keep only a hash of them to honor that).

4. Legal bases

Where privacy laws ask for a legal basis (for example in the EU and UK), we rely on: performing our agreement with you (hosting your page, running the features you use); your consent (the daily summary, the GitHub email permission, connecting private repositories, live visitors, agents, the private view); and our legitimate interests in running a safe, useful network (security, abuse prevention, suggestions and matching based on information you made public). You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings.

6. Cookies and local storage

We use only cookies needed to run the site; no advertising or analytics cookies:

  • cal_s: keeps you signed in with GitHub (up to 30 days).
  • cal_own: keeps you signed in as the owner of your page (up to 1 year; Sign out ends it).
  • cal_oauth, cal_h, cal_back: short-lived, during sign-in (minutes).
  • cal_inv, cal_grp: remember an invitation or group you arrived from, so the right person is credited (up to 30 days).

Your browser also remembers small preferences on your device (for example the city's lighting, weather or friendship toggles). We treat Global Privacy Control signals as an opt-out of any sale or sharing; since we never sell or share, nothing further changes.

7. How long we keep data

We keep your account data while your account exists. Live-visitor events are deleted after 35 days, agent activity within 24 hours, waiting connections after 90 days unseen, sign-in links and codes within minutes, and web server logs within 14 days. When you delete your account we erase your page, profile, files, friendships, connections, kudos, introductions, posts, tags, email subscription, invitations you sent, private view, traffic and agent data, and stored messages. Copies already delivered to other people (for example notes shared into a friend's vault) stay with them. We back up the database every night, and backups (including those made during maintenance) are deleted after 30 days, so a deleted account is gone from every backup within 30 days.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Control what is public in Settings (or Caldera/profile.md): directory listing, whether search engines and AI assistants may index your page, view count, topic tags, sections of your page, and automatic GitHub connections.
  • Email: unsubscribe from any email in one click, or change it in Settings.
  • Delete everything: Settings → Account → Delete my Caldera profile (GitHub sign-ups also have Caldera's GitHub access revoked), or in Obsidian run Caldera: Delete my profile from the hub.
  • Access, correction, portability or objection: email privacy@caldera.social. Depending on where you live (for example California, the EU or the UK) you have rights under local law; we respond within 30 days and will not discriminate against you for using them. You may also complain to your local data-protection authority.

9. Security

Actions from Obsidian are signed with your key and verified by the hub. Tokens, emails, private sections and agent task text are encrypted at rest. Profile pages are served in a sandbox, and connections use HTTPS. No system is perfectly secure; keep your vault key private.

10. International transfers

Caldera is run from the United States. If you use it from elsewhere, your information is processed in the United States and by the providers above, with safeguards appropriate to the transfer.

11. Children

Caldera is not for children under 13 (under 16 in the EU and UK), and they may not use it. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will delete it.

12. Self-hosted hubs

Parts of Caldera are open source. If you use a hub run by someone else, that operator's privacy policy applies to it, not this one.

13. Changes

We post updates here with a new effective date, and announce material changes on the site (and by alert or email for affected members) before they take effect.
