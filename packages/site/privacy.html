Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026 (previous version: October 18, 2026). Caldera (caldera.social, the Caldera hub and the Caldera plugin) is provided by mjonesone, LLC ("we", "us", "our").

The short version
  • Your Obsidian vault stays on your device. The plugin reads it, and some other things on your computer (git repositories and AI-tool logs, described in section 1), and sends counts plus what you choose to publish.
  • If you sign up with GitHub, we read your public GitHub profile and repositories, and only the metadata of private repositories you choose to connect. We never read the code in private repositories, and we never publish their names unless you choose to.
  • Your email is used for sign-in links, the daily summary (only if you tick the box), and a short email when someone messages you (you can turn that off). It is stored encrypted.
  • No ads, no tracking cookies, no data sales. We count visits and sign-up steps with hashes that change every day, and links in our emails say which email they came from.
  • You can delete your account and the data tied to it at any time from Settings (or from Obsidian).

What changed on October 26, 2026

This update describes things Caldera already does that the October 18 version left out or got wrong, and makes two defaults safer. In short:

  • Private repo names stay private by default. From October 20 new members' private repositories could appear by name on their public city unless they chose otherwise. That is now fixed: new members start on Anonymous, and names only show if you pick "Show with names" yourself.
  • The daily email is opt-in. The sign-up box is no longer ticked for you.
  • Deleting your account now also erases introductions, topic tags and tag edits, the days you were active, read marks, preferences, GitHub sign-in records and your Apple Wallet card data.
  • Newly described: what the Obsidian plugin reads on your computer (git repositories and Claude Code / Codex logs, both counts only); the days you were active on Caldera; the Apple Wallet card; the feedback button; Groups and @mentions; automatic setup messages from Caldera's founder; how we look up the apps and websites you list; GitHub city previews of people who are not members, and how to have one removed; the AI model we use for topic tags (DeepSeek, through OpenRouter) and exactly what it receives; the cal_dig and cal_retry cookies; links in emails; how long page-view and sign-up records are kept; and international transfers.

The October 18 version is in the history of this page.

1. What the plugin does on your device

The Caldera plugin for Obsidian runs on your computer and builds your profile there. It reads:

  • Your vault: counts (notes, words, links, active days), the tags you allow-list, the fields in Caldera/profile.md, journal lines and counts in Caldera/activity.json, notes you mark for your garden, and folder and note-type labels from a fixed generic list.
  • Git repositories on your computer (desktop only, on by default): it looks for repositories in the folders right under your home folder, in usual code folders (~/code, ~/src, ~/projects and similar) and in any folders you add, and reads git log for your own commits in the last 14 days. It never opens protected folders such as Documents, Desktop or Downloads. Only two numbers per day leave your device (lines added and lines removed); never repository names, paths, file names or code. Turn it off in Settings → Caldera → Count lines of code.
  • AI coding-tool logs (desktop only): the session logs that Claude Code (~/.claude/projects) and Codex (~/.codex/sessions) keep on your computer. Only token counts per day, by model and provider, leave your device; never prompts, replies, code or file names. Turn it off by switching off the AI usage section in Settings.
  • Your public GitHub (if you link it): public contribution and repository information, fetched from GitHub by your computer.

The plugin has no analytics or crash reporting. It talks to a hub when you publish or take an action (a friend request, a shared note, kudos, an introduction, a post), and also on its own: it republishes your page when your profile files change and every few hours, checks for new messages and alerts, checks your GitHub for changes about every 10 minutes, and fetches release notes when Obsidian starts.

Your identity key is created on your device and kept in the plugin's settings in your vault. We never receive it. If you move a GitHub sign-up into Obsidian, the hub hands its key to your vault once and deletes its copy.

GitHub city previews of people who are not members

Anyone can type a GitHub username at caldera.social/gh and see that account's public GitHub drawn as a city, with a README badge and a share picture. We build these pages from GitHub's public API only (public profile, public repositories and their commit activity), cache them for a day, and search engines may index them. They never include email addresses or private repositories. We do this because it is a useful view of public work and it shows people what Caldera does (our legitimate interest); members' usernames go to their own page instead. To have a preview removed, email privacy@caldera.social from the email address on that GitHub profile, or otherwise show us the account is yours, and say which username. We take it down within 7 days and keep only the username on a do-not-build list so it is not drawn again.

Messages

When you message another member, we store the conversation (who wrote to whom, the text and when) so both of you can read it. Messages are text only. They are encrypted on our servers with a key that stays on those servers; they are not end-to-end encrypted, and Caldera staff can access a conversation only to investigate an abuse report or to meet a legal obligation. When you report a conversation, we keep an excerpt of its recent messages with the report so it can be reviewed. We never show your email address to other members, we never sell or share messages, and we never use them for advertising or to train AI models. When someone messages you and you haven't read it within a minute, we email you a short preview (up to 200 characters) with a link back to Caldera, at most once every 30 minutes per person; unread messages can also appear in your daily summary. You can turn message emails off on the Messages page or with one click in any of them.

Automatic setup messages. Shortly after you join, and again around day 3 and day 8, Caldera sends you a few direct messages from its founder's account about setup steps you haven't finished (for example connecting Obsidian or filling in your page). They are written automatically from what is missing on your page; replies go to the founder.

Messages are kept until either person deletes the conversation (which removes it for both of you), or until an account is deleted: deleting your account erases the messages you sent, and the other person sees "Deleted user". Blocking someone stops their messages from reaching you; they aren't told.

3. Emails we send

  • Sign-in links and confirmations, when you ask for them.
  • New-message emails, when another member messages you: sent to your summary address, or to your verified GitHub email if you granted that permission. One click in the email, or the checkbox on the Messages page, turns them off.
  • The daily summary, only if you opt in by ticking the box (it is never ticked for you). It goes out only on days something happened, and every email has a one-click unsubscribe.
  • Invitations, only when a member presses "Email invite" for someone in their GitHub circle, and only to an address that person shows on their public GitHub profile. Limits apply, every invitation has a one-click unsubscribe, and unsubscribed addresses are never emailed again (we keep only a hash of them to honor that).

Links in our emails carry a short tag naming the email they came from (for example ?src=email-digest), so we can count how many visits our emails bring. The tag says which email, not who you are. Our emails contain no tracking pixels.

4. Legal bases

Where privacy laws ask for a legal basis (for example in the EU and UK), we rely on: performing our agreement with you (hosting your page, running the features you use, the Wallet card you add); your consent (the daily summary, the GitHub email permission, connecting private repositories, showing private repositories by name, live visitors, agents, the private view); and our legitimate interests in running a safe, useful network (security, abuse prevention, counting visits and sign-up steps, suggestions and matching based on information you made public, the plugin's lines-of-code and AI-token counts, and GitHub city previews of public accounts). You can withdraw consent or object at any time in Settings or by email.

6. Cookies and local storage

We use only cookies needed to run the site; no advertising or analytics cookies:

  • cal_s: keeps you signed in with GitHub (up to 30 days).
  • cal_own: keeps you signed in as the owner of your page (up to 1 year; Sign out ends it).
  • cal_oauth, cal_h, cal_back: short-lived, during sign-in (minutes).
  • cal_dig: remembers, during GitHub sign-up, whether you ticked the daily-summary box (10 minutes).
  • cal_retry: lets a failed GitHub sign-in retry itself once (2 minutes).
  • cal_inv, cal_grp: remember an invitation or group you arrived from, so the right person is credited (up to 30 days).

Your browser also remembers small preferences on your device (for example the city's lighting, weather or friendship toggles, and which new members you have already seen on the World map). The Feedback button is loaded from mjonesone.com. We treat Global Privacy Control signals as an opt-out of any sale or sharing; since we never sell or share, nothing further changes.

7. How long we keep data

We keep your account data while your account exists. Live-visitor events are deleted after 35 days, agent activity within 24 hours, waiting connections after 90 days unseen, sign-up steps after 90 days, days you were active after 12 months, Wallet card pictures after 7 days and the card log after 90 days, sign-in links and codes within minutes, and web server logs within 15 days. Profile page views are kept while your page exists. Cached GitHub city previews are refreshed daily.

When you delete your account we erase your page, profile, files, friendships and follows, connections, kudos, introductions, posts and comments, group posts, topic tags and tag edits, the days you were active, read marks and preferences, email subscription, invitations you sent, private view, traffic and agent data, GitHub sign-in records, your Apple Wallet card data, and the messages you sent. We keep only what we need to stop abuse (for example a blocked handle or a report about you) and the hashed addresses that asked never to be emailed. Copies already delivered to other people (for example notes shared into a friend's vault, or yesterday's Wallet card on a connection's phone) stay with them. We back up the database every night, and backups (including those made during maintenance) are deleted after 30 days, so a deleted account is gone from every backup within 30 days.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Control what is public in Settings (or Caldera/profile.md): directory listing, whether search engines and AI assistants may index your page, view count, topic tags, sections of your page (including AI usage), how private repositories show, and automatic GitHub connections. In Obsidian you can also turn off Count lines of code.
  • Email: unsubscribe from any email in one click, or change it in Settings.
  • Wallet card: delete it from Apple Wallet at any time; we stop updating it.
  • Delete everything: Settings → Account → Delete my Caldera profile (GitHub sign-ups also have Caldera's GitHub access revoked), or in Obsidian run Caldera: Delete my profile from the hub.
  • Not a member? Ask us to remove a GitHub city preview of your account (see above).
  • Access, correction, portability or objection: email privacy@caldera.social. Depending on where you live (for example California, the EU or the UK) you have rights under local law; we respond within 30 days and will not discriminate against you for using them. You may also complain to your local data-protection authority.

9. Security

Actions from Obsidian are signed with your key and verified by the hub. Tokens, emails, private sections and agent task text are encrypted at rest. Profile pages are served in a sandbox, and connections use HTTPS. No system is perfectly secure; keep your vault key private.

10. International transfers

Caldera is run by a US company from servers in the United States. If you use it from elsewhere (for example the EU, the UK or Switzerland), your information is transferred to and processed in the United States, and by the providers above in the countries where they work, which for the topic-tag model can include China. These countries may not have data-protection laws equivalent to yours. Where the law requires it, we rely on our providers' standard contractual clauses or other approved safeguards, or on the transfer being necessary to provide the service you asked for. Email us for details.

11. Children

Caldera is not for children under 13 (under 16 in the EU and UK), and they may not use it. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will delete it.

12. Self-hosted hubs

Parts of Caldera are open source. If you use a hub run by someone else, that operator's privacy policy applies to it, not this one.

13. Changes

We post updates here with a new effective date and a dated note of what changed. Before a change that lets us use your information in a materially new way takes effect, we announce it on the site and tell affected members by an alert or email, in advance. Changes that only describe what we already do more accurately, or that give you more protection, take effect when posted.
