Release notes

What's new

Every Caldera release. When something ships, Caldera also shows its owner a short alert inside Obsidian. Follow along with the RSS feed.

0.13.02026-10-17latest

Your city up top, and one prompt for everything

  • Your city now sits at the top of your profile: a small skyline of your repos, lit windows where you shipped this week. Click it to walk the full city.
  • One AI setup prompt instead of three. It connects every source it can find on your computer (calendar, email, AI usage, code, documents, your GitHub city), schedules one routine to keep your page fresh, and drafts your page text and garden for your OK. It's safe to paste again: it upgrades what's there and fills in what's missing.
  • My view shows what feeds your page: each source, connected or not, with a one-click copy of the setup prompt. When Caldera can track something new, it tells you to paste the prompt again.

Open My Caldera profile (ribbon icon), look at 'What feeds your page', and click Copy the setup prompt. Paste it into Claude Code, Codex or Cursor.

0.12.52026-10-17

Friend requests you can't miss

  • Fixed: a friend request could disappear before you saw it, if Obsidian closed at the wrong moment or an old copy of Caldera was still running. Open requests now live on the hub until you answer them, so they always come back.
  • The Caldera panel shows requests you've sent that are still waiting, and you can ignore a request you don't want.
  • Clearer messages when a request is accepted, and a check so you can't re-request someone who's already your friend.
0.12.42026-10-16

A city for everyone with a GitHub

  • Add your GitHub username in settings (or a GitHub line in Links) and your public repos become your city, grouped by language and refreshed every 6 hours. Until now the city needed a Caldera/city.json written by your own tools.
0.12.32026-10-16

Agent HQ parking grid, and no more stale pages

  • Parked agents line up in a tidy grid on the Agent HQ pad instead of a long row.
  • Fixed: after an update or reload, the previous version of Caldera could keep publishing your page in the background with its old look. Old copies now stop, and the hub ignores pages from an older Caldera than the last one you used.
0.12.22026-10-16

Fold the panels away

  • Tap the Underground or LIVE panel on the city to fold it down to one line, and tap again to open it. On phones they start folded.
0.12.12026-10-16

Room for your whole fleet

  • Agent HQ now parks a whole fleet in neat rows (up to 80 agents), idle drones glow dimmer, and the pad counts how many are parked and how many are waiting on you.
0.12.02026-10-16

Sign up with GitHub

  • No Obsidian? Sign up on caldera.social with GitHub: your page and your city are built from your GitHub and refresh every 6 hours. Edit it, add your sites and agents, or add private repos as padlocked buildings.
  • Already on Obsidian later? Move the profile into your vault: get a code on your account page and use Settings > Import a GitHub sign-up. Same handle, same page; your vault takes over the key.
0.11.02026-10-16

The Underground: your vault under the city

  • New Underground button on the city: the ground turns to glass and your Obsidian vault appears below as a glowing network. Notes are nodes (bigger = more notes link to them), links are tunnels, and depth is age.
  • Notes about a project hang under its district, with roots up into its buildings, so you can see which projects have deep knowledge behind them.
  • Tap a note to see its links and open it. Notes you edit pulse light through their connections. Unconnected notes are counted so you can link them up.
  • Visitors only see the shape: no titles, except notes you've published to your garden.
0.10.32026-10-16

Easier updates

  • When a newer Caldera is out, What's new tells you which version you have and opens Community plugins so you can update in one click.
0.10.22026-10-16

Full-screen city

  • New Full screen button on the city map (Esc or Exit to leave). Works on the public page, your private address and the My profile tab.
  • On phones, the live panel folds down to one line; tap it to open.
0.10.12026-10-16

Tidier Agent HQ

  • Agents parked at Agent HQ no longer pile their labels on top of each other: the pad says how many are waiting on you, and you tap a drone for details.
0.10.02026-10-16

Your AI agents, flying over the city

  • Every Claude Code agent you run becomes a drone over the repo it's working in: a beam into the roof while it edits, circling while it thinks, parked at Agent HQ blinking amber when it's waiting on you.
  • Tap a drone to see what it's working on, its last step and tokens today (My view and your private address only). Your public city shows anonymous drones, never what they're doing.
  • Set it up in Settings > AI agents on your city: copy the prompt into Claude Code on each computer. A tiny hook reports the repo and a short task title; task details are encrypted on the hub.
0.9.12026-10-16

Every car is a real visitor

  • Once your sites send live traffic, the city's cars are your visitors: one car for each person on that site right now. It pulls out of their district when they arrive and fades away when they leave.
  • The live panel no longer scrolls: busy districts get a row, quiet ones share a single line, and a hint shows if no visits have arrived yet.
0.9.02026-10-16

Live traffic in your city

  • Your city is now live: every visit to your own websites rides out of that project's district as a light, signups pulse pink and sales fire a gold beam.
  • Each district gets a one-line snippet (Settings > Live traffic on your city). Paste it into your site and the traffic appears within seconds. No cookies, and visitors are only counted, never identified.
  • Your public city only shows how busy each district is. The numbers (live visitors, views, signups, sales) stay in My view and your private address.
0.8.32026-10-16

Your city, on the Grid

  • New default look for your city: Grid mode. Black glass towers outlined in neon (one color per language), glowing window strips, a lit grid floor, and light cycles racing the streets with light trails.
  • Your tallest building sends a beam of light into the sky.
  • Prefer daylight? The button cycles Grid, Day, Dusk and Night.
0.8.22026-10-16

A much better-looking city

  • Your city got a full visual upgrade: it now sits on a little island with a river, sidewalks, lane markings and parks.
  • Buildings have real shading and shadows. Big projects become stepped skyscrapers, with rooftop details: water towers, solar panels, roof gardens, a helipad, and a blinking antenna on your tallest.
  • Street lamps glow at night, cars have headlights, and the crane arms slowly swing. The sky switches between day, dusk and night, with clouds or stars.
  • It's lighter to run, so it stays smooth on phones.
0.8.12026-10-16

Your whole city, private repos locked

  • Your public city now shows every repo, so visitors see the real size of what you're building.
  • Private repos are locked glass buildings with a padlock: no name, language, commit history, link or exact numbers. Tapping one just says it's private.
  • Projects that aren't on your public profile don't show their names; their buildings sit in one district called Private.
0.8.02026-10-16

Caldera City: your GitHub as a city

  • A new City page next to your profile turns your GitHub into a little isometric city. Each project is a district and each repo a building: taller means more commits this year, lit windows mean commits this week, and a crane means it's being built right now. Cars are commit traffic.
  • Your Obsidian notes are the houses: every note that links to a project's note becomes a house in that district. Tap a building for its 12-week commit history, last push and the notes about it.
  • Private repos stay private: visitors see public repos and the projects already on your profile. Your full city, every repo included, shows only in the My profile tab (and on your private address).
  • Set it up by asking your AI agent to write Caldera/city.json (the setup prompt now explains how), then publish. Drag, scroll or pinch to explore; there's a day/night switch.
0.7.12026-10-16

Your private view in a browser, over Tailscale

  • Self-hosting a hub on your Tailscale network? Turn on 'Private web address (Tailscale)' in Caldera settings and your private sections open in a browser on any of your Tailscale devices, with a My view / Public view switch. Only your own Tailscale account can open it, and it's stored encrypted.
  • The views chart in My view now always shows the last 14 days.
0.7.02026-10-15

Your profile, inside Obsidian

  • New My profile tab: click the person icon in the left ribbon (or run 'Caldera: Open my profile in Obsidian'). Your page opens as a tab, and you can click through to your garden and notes.
  • My view is built from your vault right now and adds sections only you can see, never published: who visited since you last looked, what they read, gravity and new friends; today as it happens; what you were doing a week, a month and a year ago; an old note brought back every few hours; your patterns; and goals from Caldera/Goals.md.
  • Public view shows your live page exactly as visitors see it.
  • No login needed: your vault's key signs you in, so only you can see the private part.
  • Optional: open it automatically when Obsidian starts (Caldera settings, My profile tab).
0.6.12026-10-15

Same nav everywhere, and adding friends in one click

  • Every profile and garden page has an 'Add @handle as friend' button. It opens Obsidian and sends the request after you confirm.

  • Add a friend by handle or by pasting their profile link. Friend requests now show who sent them and stay put if you restart Obsidian.
0.6.02026-10-15

Your garden home

  • Your garden page now opens with 'How this garden works': notes planted, links from other gardens, gravity, help credits (and how many are held in bounties), your open asks and offers, friends and your weekly rank. Read live from the hub every time someone visits.
  • Plain explanations on the page of how gravity (goodwill friends give) and help credits (bounties on asks) work, and how other gardens can link your notes.
  • The Garden section on your profile now links straight to your garden home.
  • The AI setup prompts walk new users through it: which sections to turn on, what gravity and credits are, and drafting their first asks and offers.
0.5.42026-10-12

Your garden grows itself from your daily journal

  • Each public project your journal keeps mentioning gets its own garden page, with a dated entry every day it comes up. Pages start as seedlings and become budding (5 days) and evergreen (20 days) on their own.
  • Recurring themes get a page too, but only when they're distinctive (not on nearly every day, and not a copy of another theme).
  • Wrote a garden note with the same name as a project? Your words stay on top and the journal log appears underneath under 'From my journal'.
  • Only journal text that is already public (after the privacy filter) is used. Journal entries are kept in the plugin's own data, so pages keep growing past the 45-day journal window.
  • Your page now republishes when a daily note's journal changes, and catches up when you open Obsidian after more than 6 hours away.
  • Turn it off with garden_journal: false in Caldera/profile.md or Settings -> Caldera.

Nothing to do if your daily journal is set up. Name projects the way they appear in your profile's projects list and they get pages.

0.5.02026-10-12

Gardens: grow notes in public, linked to other people's gardens

  • Plant any note in your garden and it becomes a full public page on your profile. Put it in a Garden folder, or run 'Caldera: Plant this note in my garden'. Nothing else in your vault is published.
  • Every garden note shows how finished it is: seedling, budding or evergreen (frontmatter stage:). Your profile gets a Garden section and a garden index you can filter by stage.
  • Choose a license per note (license:). The default is all rights reserved and not licensed for AI training; cc-by, cc-by-nc, cc0 and ai-licensable are available.
  • Signed authorship: every published note is hashed and listed in your signed profile manifest, so anyone can check who wrote it and when.
  • Link to a friend's note with [[@handle/Their note]]. Their note shows 'Linked from other gardens' with a link back to yours.
  • Private by default: email addresses and phone numbers are removed, %% comments %% never leave your vault, and links to notes outside the garden show as plain text. 'Caldera: Preview my garden' lists every line the privacy check flags before anything goes public.
  • New: caldera.social/gardens shows notes recently planted or tended across all listed gardens. Already have a garden on Quartz or Obsidian Publish? Add garden_url to Caldera/profile.md and your page links to it.

Create a folder called Garden in your vault (or run 'Caldera: Plant this note in my garden' on a note), then run 'Caldera: Preview my garden' and 'Caldera: Publish my profile'.

Fuller pages out of the box: AI usage, Code, What it knows and a vault graph

  • Code: add your GitHub profile to your links and your page shows commits per week from your public contribution graph.

0.3.132026-10-11

Fix: first profile publishes in about a second (was up to 90 seconds)

  • Creating your profile from the Welcome screen could sit on 'Publishing...' for up to a minute and a half while Caldera waited for Obsidian to finish indexing. It now checks directly and publishes right away. Every later publish is faster too.
  • If publishing fails, the Welcome screen now says why, and it comes back next time you open Obsidian until your profile is live.

If you installed Caldera and your profile never appeared, run 'Caldera: Set up my profile (welcome)' from the command palette.

0.3.22026-10-11

Stronger privacy: journal lines that look sensitive are never published

  • Caldera now checks every journal line before publishing and drops any that mention other people's names, meetings or their times, places and addresses, secret or unannounced work, money, legal, health, HR or family matters, or contact details. Only names you already made public on your page (your projects, topics and links) are allowed.
  • All AI prompts carry one stricter privacy policy: never reveal who you met, where or when, secret projects, or anything that could get someone in trouble. When unsure, leave it out.
  • New Learn pages at caldera.social/learn answer common questions about Obsidian profiles, private sharing and AI agents.

Nothing to do: the filter runs automatically on your next publish.
