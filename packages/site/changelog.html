0.22.02026-10-18

Your own sections: custom modules

  • Custom modules: add a note to Caldera/modules/ (for example Caldera/modules/Reading list.md) and it becomes a section on your page, titled with the file name. Write ordinary Markdown: lists, checkboxes, tables, quotes, code, links and images from your vault.
  • Put a module in the main column (default) or the right-hand column with place: side, order them with order: 10, and keep drafts private with publish: false. Up to 12 modules; the page republishes about a minute after you save.
  • Or just ask your AI agent: "add a Talks module with my last five talks". The setup prompt (now version 6), the edit prompt, profile.md and the guide all explain how.
  • Safe by design: no HTML, scripts or embeds, and email addresses and phone numbers are removed automatically. Everything in a module is public.
  • Tables now work in Markdown on Caldera pages.
0.21.02026-10-18

Messages, private repos your way, and the Terms

  • Messages: when you have unread messages on Caldera, Obsidian shows a notice. Click it to open your Messages, already signed in.
  • Private repos on my city: choose how private repos show to visitors. Show as padlocked (as before), Anonymous (one separate Private district of identical padlocks, no activity, a rough size instead of a count) or Hidden (no trace). Set it in Caldera's settings, in Caldera/profile.md (privateRepos: show, anonymous or hidden), or on caldera.social/settings. Your private view always shows everything.
  • The Welcome form now notes the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy (mjonesone, LLC).
  • The setup prompt is version 5: it asks which private-repo level you want.

To pick your private-repo level, ask your AI: "Set privateRepos in my Caldera/profile.md to anonymous", or open Settings > Caldera.

Friends who work on the same repos

  • Members who both work on the same public GitHub repo now become friends automatically, with the reason shown: "both work on owner/repo" on the friend button, in your friends list, in the daily email, and on the World map. A fork counts for the repo it came from. Private repos are never read.

  • The side menu links companies named in your timeline to their sites, and a hand-picked group shows how many items it lists, not how many notes are in the folder.
  • The side menu scrolls without a visible scrollbar.

0.19.12026-10-17

Real weather, people on foot, the day's traces

  • The sky over every city now matches the weather where you are: clear, partly cloudy, overcast, fog, drizzle, rain, storms with lightning, or snow, with the wind moving the clouds and the rain. Dawn and dusk follow your real sunrise and sunset. Caldera looks up the weather from your connection each time and keeps nothing about where you are.
  • Pick a different sky with the new Weather button: Auto (your weather), Clear, Rain, Snow or Fog. Your browser remembers the choice.
  • Visitors now parachute down at street scale, the size of the cars, then walk along the street to a building's door and go in. Signups and sales walk in too, after their celebration.
  • People who have left walk back out as faint ghosts and fade at the edge of the district. When you open your city, the last half hour of departures replays in the first 20 seconds, so it feels alive straight away.
  • Worn paths: faint footprints lead to your busiest buildings, darker with more visitors today and fading through the day. They reset at your midnight, and they show in rain and snow too.
  • Your city shows how many people visited today and how many are on your sites now.
  • Clouds drift smoothly and never jump or reset, including when you resize the pop-out.
  • Only you, on your own city: if live visitors or AI-agent drones aren't set up yet, a quiet row under the map shows how, with each site's one-line snippet, a Copy button and a prompt for your AI. Visitors never see it. It goes away when your first visit arrives, or when you dismiss it.

Open your city (caldera.social/@you/city.html or My view in Obsidian). The weather button sits next to the lighting button.

0.18.02026-10-17

Watch your city all day

  • Pop out: a new button on the city opens it alone in a small window you can park in a corner of your screen. In Chrome and Edge, click "Keep on top" and it floats over everything (picture-in-picture). It works on your public page and on your private address. In Obsidian, "Pop out my city" (button in My profile, or the command) opens your private city in a small window that stays on top in the lower-left corner.
  • The city follows your clock: night, dawn, day, dusk and evening, blending slowly through the day. The sky button cycles Auto, Grid, Dawn, Day, Dusk, Evening and Night, and your choice is remembered where your browser allows it. The city picture on your profile follows the time of day too.
  • Arrivals: every new visitor on one of your sites parachutes into its district, lands and walks into a building. A signup is a beam of light, confetti and a toast; a sale is a burst of gold coins. When lots arrive at once they queue, with a +N counter. Public pages show the same arrivals, without visitor details or private project names. Sound is off by default; turn it on with the speaker button.
  • GitHub from the city: click a public repo's name label, or double-click its building, to open it on GitHub. In the pop-out, one click opens the repo. Your GitHub profile is linked next to the city's title. Private and padlocked buildings never link.
  • Group towns and the People directory link each member's city, so you can go from a person to their city to their repos.

Open your city and click Pop out. In Chrome or Edge, hover the small window and click Keep on top. In Obsidian: My profile > the picture-in-picture button, or the command "Caldera: Pop out my city".
